PITCH STEP 01 / 04

The Solution

The spending rules live in the agent's own smart account, not in the agent.

  • Agents pay for APIs over x402 in USDC, each with a ZK passport credential: one per agent, anti-Sybil by nullifier.
  • Every payment passes the account's checks: allowed payee, cap per period, not revoked.
  • A prompt-injected agent can sign anything. The network still refuses it.

Why it's the best fix:it works even when the agent itself is compromised.

PITCH STEP 02 / 04

PMF

Agents with wallets are shipping now, and prompt injection is the top risk for them.

  • Owners: today you either babysit every payment or trust a cap that a hijacked agent bypasses.
  • Sellers: APIs can't open free tiers or sell to agents without bot farms eating them.
  • Ecosystem: x402 and MPP are live on Stellar; what's missing is bounded authority for agents.

Top risk: OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2025, LLM01 Prompt Injection.

Why care:one injected page is enough to drain an agent's wallet. Ours lost zero.

PITCH STEP 03 / 04

Technical Workflow

Prove. Bind. Enforce. With the official x402 facilitator, unchanged.

  • Prove: a Groth16 ZK passport made on the client in about 2 s, verified on BN254 in Soroban.
  • Bind: PassportFactory verifies the proof and deploys the agent's smart account in one tx.
  • Enforce: PassportAccount.__check_auth checks passport, payee, cap and revocation on every USDC transfer; the ledger rejects anything else (real FAILED txs on testnet).

Why it will succeed:it's live on testnet today, with 62 contract tests and a public demo anyone can try.

Three steps. Prove, bind, enforce. The key is that enforcement runs inside __check_auth, so the official facilitator rejects at simulation and the ledger rejects if anyone skips it.

Prior work is declared: the ZK passport comes from open-stellar-passport, and Open Stellar had the marketplace with checks in the backend. Here we moved them onchain.
